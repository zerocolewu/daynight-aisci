Analysis:
============================================================

1. Overall Ranking (Test RMSE, lower is better):
   1st: Trimer (c)       50.19 RMSE (-2.51 vs baseline, -4.77%)
   2nd: Augmented (e)    50.52 RMSE (-2.18 vs baseline, -4.14%)
   3rd: Dimer (b)        51.29 RMSE (-1.41 vs baseline, -2.68%)
   4th: Monomer (a)      52.71 RMSE (baseline)
   5th: Cyclized (d)     53.28 RMSE (+0.57 vs baseline, +1.09%)

2. Multimer Transformations Help:
   - Dimer vs Monomer: -1.41 RMSE (2.68% improvement)
     Doubling the repeat units provides a moderate improvement by exposing
     the model to inter-unit connectivity patterns.
   - Trimer vs Monomer: -2.51 RMSE (4.77% improvement)
     Three repeat units provide the best performance, capturing more of
     the polymer's local structural environment. This is consistent with
     GRIN's finding that trimer is the minimal sufficient augmentation.
   - Trimer vs Dimer: -1.10 RMSE (additional improvement from 3rd unit)
     The improvement from dimer to trimer is substantial, suggesting that
     the third repeat unit adds meaningful structural information.

3. Cyclization Effect:
   - Cyclized trimer vs Trimer: +3.09 RMSE (WORSENED significantly)
   - Cyclized trimer vs Monomer: +0.57 RMSE (even worse than baseline)
   - The cyclized trimer showed the lowest training RMSE (26.53) but the
     highest test RMSE (53.28), indicating severe overfitting.
   - The periodic bond creates a shortcut in the graph that may confuse
     the GIN aggregation, leading to poor generalization.
   - The model trained longer (94 epochs vs 45 for trimer) and still
     overfit, suggesting the periodic edge introduces noise rather than
     useful periodicity information with this GIN architecture.

4. Feature Augmentation Effect:
   - Augmented trimer vs Trimer: +0.33 RMSE (slightly worse)
   - Augmented trimer vs Monomer: -2.18 RMSE (4.14% improvement)
   - Adding the repeat_unit_index feature (3-dim one-hot) did not help
     compared to plain trimer. The GIN architecture with mean pooling
     may not effectively leverage this positional information.
   - The feature slightly increased the feature dimension (30->33) without
     providing proportional benefit, suggesting the model can implicitly
     learn RU boundaries from the graph structure itself.

5. Convergence Analysis:
   - Monomer: slowest convergence (best_epoch=30), suggesting the model
     needs more epochs to learn from less informative input.
   - Dimer/Trimer/Augmented: fast convergence (best_epoch=14-16),
     indicating multimer representations provide stronger learning signal.
   - Cyclized: slow convergence (best_epoch=64) with longest training
     (94 epochs), further evidence of the periodic edge being problematic.

6. Computational Cost:
   - Graph size scales linearly: monomer (11.2 nodes) -> dimer (22.5) -> trimer (33.7)
   - Despite larger graphs, training time did not increase proportionally
     due to earlier convergence in multimer experiments.
   - Cyclized trimer took the longest (339.4s) due to more training epochs.

7. Overfitting Analysis:
   - Monomer shows moderate overfitting: train=36.4, val=41.4, test=52.7
   - Multimers (dimer/trimer/augmented) show similar overfitting patterns
   - Cyclized trimer shows the MOST overfitting: train=26.5, val=39.6, test=53.3
     This large gap between train and test performance is a clear sign of
     overfitting, likely due to the periodic bond creating graph-level
     artifacts that don't generalize.

Conclusions:
- Best transformation: Trimer (3 repeat units) with standard features
- Multimer augmentation consistently improves Tg prediction (2-5%)
- Cyclization with current GIN architecture hurts performance
- Feature augmentation (repeat unit index) provides marginal benefit
- The results validate GRIN's approach of using trimer as the minimal
  sufficient structural representation for polymer property prediction
